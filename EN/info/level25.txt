── UTF-16 ────────────────────────────────────────────────
Not everything unreadable is a cipher — sometimes it's just a
different text encoding. UTF-16 stores each code unit in TWO
bytes (LE or BE), often with a BOM (FF FE / FE FF) up front.
In a hex view you'll see a 00 after every ASCII letter.

Windows and PowerShell love UTF-16LE — a common obfuscation is
a UTF-16 + base64 PowerShell payload (`-EncodedCommand`).

  iconv -f UTF-16LE -t UTF-8 file        transcode
iconv converts between hundreds of charsets; picking the right
source encoding is the whole trick.
